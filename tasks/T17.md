# T17 — Resolve the roof discrepancy

**Depends on:** T03 (source images) · **Blocks:** T11's roof, T09's roof/height invariants

## Goal

Determine the house's **actual roof geometry**, and explain why the published figures do
not close. Investigation, not construction — the deliverable is a documented answer.

## The discrepancy

Read "OPEN DISCREPANCY" in `README.md` first. In short: footprint 17.10 × 9.03 m is
confirmed (= 154.4 m², matching published 154.42). A symmetric gable at the published
**35°** springing from the section's **+2.88 m** wall plate puts the ridge at **+6.04 m**.
The section prints **+6.77 m**. Alternatives do not close it: attic floor +3.04 → +6.20;
attic floor + 29 cm knee wall → +6.49.

Two figures agree with each other but not with the footprint:

| Derived from | Implied roof span |
|---|---|
| Rise 3.89 m at 35° | 11.11 m |
| Roof area 216.8 m² at 35°, ridge ~17.10 m | 10.39 m |
| **Footprint** | **9.03 m** |

Implied pitch from the section's own numbers: **40.7°**, not 35°.

The vertical chain is internally consistent — 6.77 − (−0.32) = 7.09 m matches the
published building height exactly. So the conflict is between that chain and the
pitch/footprint pair, and at least one published figure is wrong or is being misread.

## Method

### 1. Measure the roof triangle directly

`data/source/section.png` and the **gable-end elevations** show the roof triangle. Measure
it in pixels and compute the apex angle. The gable elevation is the better source: it
shows the full triangle in true shape, uncluttered.

Calibrate using a known dimension in the same image — the 9.03 m building width or the
2.70 m storey height — then read the pitch off the measured rise and run.

Elevations are photorealistic renders rather than dimensioned orthographic drawings (T03's
finding), so treat any angle measured from them as **corroborating evidence, not proof**.
The section is dimensioned and is the primary source, despite being only 400×300 px.

### 2. Test the candidate explanations

- **Pitch is not 35°.** Does the measured angle come out near 40.7°? If so, 35° is likely
  a rounded or erroneous marketing figure. Check whether 40.7° also reproduces the
  216.8 m² roof area with a plausible overhang.
- **Roof is not a simple symmetric gable.** Look for knee walls above the wall plate, a
  changed slope (mansard-like), dormers, or asymmetric spans. The published *"two-slope"*
  description argues against this, but verify rather than assume.
- **Datum mismatch.** Is +6.77 the ridge *board*, the roof *surface*, or something else?
  Is +2.88 the wall plate or the ground-floor ceiling? A 0.73 m gap is large for a datum
  slip, but check.
- **Large overhangs.** ~0.42 m all round reconciles the roof *area* but not the *rise*.
  Overhangs cannot explain a ridge-height gap, since the ridge is unaffected by them —
  confirm this reasoning rather than treating overhangs as a candidate fix.

### 3. Cross-check against the attic

The attic banding contours (`140` / `220`, traced in T05) are a strong independent
constraint: their positions in plan are a direct function of pitch and knee-wall height.
Whichever pitch you conclude must place those contours where the attic plan draws them.
This is the best available check and is largely independent of the section.

Likewise, the attic usable areas (14.67 + 18.21 = 32.88 m²) are only reproducible at the
correct pitch. A wrong pitch moves the 1.4 m contour and changes those areas measurably.

## Hard rules

- **Do not resolve this by choosing whichever input makes the arithmetic work.** The point
  is to find out which published figure is wrong and why.
- **Do not force the ridge to +6.77 m in the eventual model.** See T09 and T11: a forced
  ridge turns the height invariant into a tautology and deletes the roof check.
- If the evidence is genuinely insufficient to decide, **say so**. Report the most likely
  answer, its confidence, and what would be needed to settle it. An honest "40.7° fits the
  section and the attic contours, but the images are too coarse to exclude 39–42°" is far
  more useful than false precision.

## Deliverables

- `docs/roof-resolution.md` — measurements taken, pixel calibration method, angle(s)
  computed, each candidate explanation tested and its verdict, and a recommended roof
  geometry with a stated confidence level
- A recommended `pitch_deg` and `eaves_overhang` for `spec/meta.json`, **with the caveat
  that both are `derived: true`** if they contradict the published 35°
- An explicit statement of which published figure(s) you conclude are wrong

## Report back

The recommended pitch, your confidence, and whether the attic contour cross-check
supports or contradicts it.
