## What makes the attic different

### Sloped ceilings change what "area" means

The attic sits under a 35° gable roof, so usable area is height-dependent under Polish
convention:

| Clear height | Counted as |
|---|---|
| < 1.4 m | 0% — excluded entirely |
| 1.4 – 2.2 m | 50% |
| > 2.2 m | 100% |

These thresholds are confirmed: Archon states PN-ISO 9836, and `plan_attic.png` prints
ceiling-height contour lines labelled `140` and `220` — trace those contours directly
rather than recomputing them from the roof, then cross-check against the roof geometry.

This means **you cannot check attic room areas by measuring floor polygons.** The
geometry kernel needs the roof surface to compute the height bands. Coordinate with T07
and T11: the attic area computation is a function of `(floor polygon, roof geometry)`,
not of the floor polygon alone.

Implement the banding rule in `src/kotewki/quantities.py` as
`usable_area_sloped(polygon, roof, floor_elevation)` and unit-test it against a synthetic
case with a known answer before trusting it on real data.

### Published attic figures

| Room | Usable area | Floor area (podłóg) |
|---|---|---|
| Antresola (mezzanine) | 14.51 m² | 29.40 m² |
| Strych ocieplony | 14.67 m² | — |
| Strych ocieplony | 18.21 m² | 31.31 m² |
| Schody (stairs) | 3.64 m² | — |

Use the publisher's verbatim names (`Strych ocieplony`, not `Poddasze`) — T10 matches on
them. The parenthesised *powierzchnia podłóg* figures are a **second independent
constraint per room**: raw floor area ignoring ceiling height, so they check your floor
polygon directly without the banding rule confounding it. Diagnostically valuable — if
floor area passes and usable area fails, the banding rule is wrong, not the geometry.

**Stairs are excluded from "usable area" but included in "net area"** — see the
reconciliation in `README.md`. Tag the stairs room so T09 can include/exclude it
correctly per invariant. Getting this wrong produces a 3.64 m² error that looks like a
geometry bug and is not one.

Note `14.67 + 18.21 = 32.88` — this matches the published "attic area" exactly, confirming
that the two `Poddasze` entries are the unconditioned attic and `Antresola` is counted
separately. Use that as a transcription check.

### The section drawing exists — use it

`data/source/section.png` is the authority on vertical geometry, and T03 confirmed it is
legible. Transcribed values, now **source fact rather than derived**:

| | |
|---|---|
| Terrain | −0.32 m |
| Ground floor | ±0.00 m |
| Eave / wall plate top | +2.88 m |
| Attic floor | +3.04 m |
| Ridge | +6.77 m |
| Clear heights | 2.70 m ground, 2.73 m attic |
| Knee wall | 29 cm |

Ridge − terrain = 7.09 m, matching the published building height exactly — so T09's height
invariant is a genuine independent check, not a solve.

**Caveat:** the section is only 400×300 px and there is no larger variant. It is legible
for the five printed level marks above, but it is the weakest artifact in the set. Any
vertical value you infer from it *beyond* those printed marks must be `derived: true` and
flagged in your report.

### Units

The plans print **centimetres**; the spec stores millimetres. Chains go into `_cm` fields
verbatim and the loader converts. Read "Units" in `README.md` first.
